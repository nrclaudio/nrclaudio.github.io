About Me

Researcher. Problem-solver. Creative.

I'm a researcher with a background in biotechnology and a PhD in computational biology. I focus on developing generative models and graph-based approaches to understand biological systems, particularly how gene expression and metabolism interact in space and disease.

I've designed deep learning models for multi-omics integration, worked with large-scale single-cell and spatial data, and explored optimal transport to uncover mechanisms behind cellular communication. My goal is to build AI tools that push the frontiers of biomedical discovery.

Generative Modeling for Metabolite Prediction

Designed a graph-based model using enzyme expression to infer metabolite abundance across subsystems, aiming to apply this framework to spatial transcriptomics.

Modeling Cell-Cell Communication

Developed a predictive model for ligand-receptor signaling using NicheNet and metabolomic cues, integrating spatial domain knowledge to improve interaction specificity.

NicheNet Multi-Omics Biological Graphs

spVIPES: Spatial Variational Inference for Perturbation Effects

I designed spVIPES, a model that disentangles biological variation from perturbation effects in spatial transcriptomics. The architecture combines variational inference with a novel optimal transport alignment to compare treated vs. control tissues at the single-spot level.
